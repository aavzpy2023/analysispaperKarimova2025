\subsection{Conclusions and future directions}

This study critically re-evaluates the methodological requirements for effective molecular property prediction in low-data regimes, yielding three primary conclusions. First, for small-scale datasets, classical machine learning ensembles leveraging the explicit inductive bias of 2D topological fingerprints match or exceed the predictive utility of complex deep learning architectures and computationally expensive 3D descriptors. Second, the deployment of a rigorous nested cross-validation framework is imperative to prevent optimistic performance bias and ensure the true generalizability of the selected models. Finally, integrating ligand-based machine learning predictions with structure-based molecular docking establishes a robust consensus filtering protocol. This orthogonal validation effectively mitigates the false-positive rates inherent to standalone screening methods, ensuring that only high-confidence, structurally viable candidates are prioritized for therapeutic repurposing.
