\section{Results}

\subsection{Comparative Performance of Classical ML Across Feature Spaces}

To establish a solid baseline for TgDHFR inhibitor prediction, we evaluated classical machine learning ensembles across four distinct molecular representations using a rigorous \TotalOuterFolds-fold nested cross-validation framework. The purely structural representation utilizing Morgan fingerprints (ECFP4, 2048 bits) without additional physicochemical descriptors yielded the highest predictive performance ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean \pm \MorganRTwoStd$, MAE = \MorganMaeMean). Within this feature space, a stacked ensemble comprising \MorganBestArchitecture\ emerged as the most frequently selected architecture during the inner cross-validation phase.

Increasing feature complexity did not translate to improved predictive accuracy. The integration of RDKit 2D descriptors with Morgan fingerprints (analogous to the original study's ``2D/FP'' model) resulted in an $R^2$ of $\RdkitTwoDFpRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDFpRTwoStd$ and an MAE of \RdkitTwoDFpMaeMean. Furthermore, the most complex representation, which incorporated 3D geometric descriptors derived from ETKDGv3 embedding and MMFF94s optimization (analogous to the ``2D/3D/FP'' model), achieved an $R^2$ of $\RdkitTwoDThreeDFpRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDThreeDFpRTwoStd$ and an MAE of \RdkitTwoDThreeDFpMaeMean. A paired t-test between the purely structural Morgan fingerprint model and the highly engineered 2D/3D/FP model revealed no statistically significant difference in performance ($t = \PairedTTestStat$, $p = \PairedTTestP$). Conversely, relying solely on 2D descriptors yielded the lowest performance across all configurations ($R^2 = \RdkitTwoDRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDRTwoStd$, MAE = \RdkitTwoDMaeMean), indicating that structural fingerprints are the primary drivers of predictive power for this specific dataset.

To explicitly contextualize the computational cost-benefit ratio of advanced statistical techniques, we benchmarked our highest-performing classical model against the incremental progression reported in the original study (Table~\ref{tab:methodological_comparison}). Our classical stacking ensemble on Morgan features ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean$) performed comparably to their unselected 2D/3D/FP baseline model ($R^2 = \PaperBaselineRTwo$), with no statistically significant difference ($t = \TStatMorganVsPaperBaseline$, $p = \PValueMorganVsPaperBaseline$). 

However, this baseline classical approach fell short of the original study's final data-augmented deep neural network ensemble ($R^2 = \PaperFinalRTwo$, $t = \TStatMorganVsPaperFinal$, $p < 0.001$). These comparative results critically isolate the true source of predictive gains in this low-data regime. They demonstrate that achieving a competitive $R^2 \approx 0.75$ does not require computationally expensive 3D descriptors or complex neural network architectures; it can be accomplished "out-of-the-box" using simple 2D fingerprints and classical ML algorithms. Consequently, the $\approx 0.10$ gap in $R^2$ performance between our baseline and the original study's final model directly represents the massive computational cost and algorithmic complexity required for synthetic data augmentation.

\subsection{Benchmarking Tree-Based Ensembles Against Graph Neural Networks}

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) represent the current state-of-the-art in molecular property prediction by learning representations directly from molecular graphs. However, their efficacy is highly dependent on dataset size. To evaluate whether deep learning architectures inherently provide an advantage for this specific TgDHFR dataset (\NCompounds\ compounds), we benchmarked our best-performing classical ensemble against a Directed Message Passing Neural Network (D-MPNN) implemented via ChemProp.

The D-MPNN yielded an $R^2$ of \ChemPropDMPNNRTwoMean\ (95\% CI [\ChemPropDMPNNCILow, \ChemPropDMPNNCIHigh]) and a MAE of \ChemPropDMPNNMae. This performance falls substantially below that of the classical stacking ensemble utilizing Morgan fingerprints ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean$). The performance degradation exhibited by the GNN is a direct consequence of data scarcity; with only \NCompounds\ training instances, the D-MPNN lacks sufficient data volume to learn graph convolutions that outperform pre-computed structural fingerprints. 

Crucially, the collapse of this state-of-the-art representation learning algorithm provides critical contextual validation for the methodologies employed by \cite{karimova2025}. The fact that an advanced D-MPNN significantly underperforms on this raw dataset inherently explains why the original study was compelled to deploy heavy statistical interventions—such as Gaussian data augmentation and extensive DNN tuning—to stabilize training and force a high $R^2$ (\PaperFinalRTwo, $p < 0.001$). These findings underscore our central thesis: when unaugmented, data-limited baselines are evaluated, architectural complexity alone fails to guarantee superior predictive accuracy. Instead, classical tree-based ensembles operating on fixed molecular descriptors remain the most robust, parsimonious, and computationally efficient choice. The comparative performance across all baseline models and the GNN is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:gnn_comparison}.

\subsection{Virtual Screening and Prioritization of FDA-Approved Candidates}

To identify potential repurposing candidates, the optimized classical machine learning model was deployed to screen a filtered library of FDA-approved drugs. The predictive reliability of the model was first validated against known TgDHFR inhibitors present in the dataset. The model demonstrated excellent agreement with experimental values, predicting a pIC$_{50}$ of 6.4226 for pyrimethamine (experimental: 6.56, absolute error: 0.1374) and 5.6590 for trimethoprim (experimental: 5.57, absolute error: 0.0890). 

The virtual screening prioritized candidates based on absolute predicted potency alongside established ligand efficiency metrics, including Ligand Efficiency (LE), Binding Efficiency Index (BEI), Lipophilic Ligand Efficiency (LLE), and Surface Efficiency Index (SEI). Table~\ref{tab:fda_candidates} details the top 10 predicted compounds. Antifolate analogs, including folic acid, methotrexate, and pralatrexate, exhibited the highest absolute predicted affinities (pIC$_{50} > 7.6$). However, absolute potency often scales with molecular weight, which can obscure the intrinsic binding quality of the scaffold. 

When evaluated through the lens of heavy-atom efficiency, smaller molecules demonstrated superior binding profiles. Pyrimethamine (LE = 0.52, BEI = 25.82) and the potassium-sparing diuretic triamterene (LE = 0.49, BEI = 27.09) emerged as highly efficient binders. Triamterene, in particular, represents a compelling repurposing candidate, as it achieves a high predicted pIC$_{50}$ of 6.8599 while maintaining exceptional ligand and binding efficiency indices, suggesting a highly optimized interaction with the TgDHFR active site relative to its molecular size.

\subsection{Integrated ML--Docking Consensus Filtering and False-Positive Mitigation}

To mitigate the false-positive rates inherent to standalone virtual screening methods, we implemented an orthogonal consensus filtering pipeline that integrates ligand-based ML predictions with physics-based molecular docking. We assessed the agreement between the ML-predicted pIC$_{50}$ values and AutoDock Vina binding energies across the prioritized subset, yielding a Pearson correlation coefficient of $r = \DockPearsonR$ ($p = \DockPearsonP$). 

Crucially, this consensus approach effectively eliminated false-positive candidates that might otherwise be prioritized by isolated screening protocols. For example, compounds such as bisacodyl were assigned low predicted affinities by our classical ML model and correspondingly exhibited weak binding energies during structural validation. By confirming these molecules as true negatives, the integrated filter prevents the misallocation of experimental resources toward artifacts of single-method screening.

The orthogonal docking step also served to validate high-potential candidates, successfully identifying \DockNValidated\ consensus hits from the \DockNTotal\ evaluated compounds that satisfied both the ML potency threshold and stringent docking energy criteria. The most promising consensus candidate, \DockBestCandidate, demonstrated a strong ML-predicted pIC$_{50}$ of \DockBestMLpIC\ coupled with a highly favorable docking score of \DockBestDockScore\ kcal/mol. 

These results definitively establish that our lightweight, classical pipeline not only matches the statistical performance of unaugmented deep learning models, but is also fully capable of discovering biologically viable and physics-validated inhibitors. By successfully isolating high-confidence repurposing candidates without relying on synthetic data generation or expensive 3D feature engineering, this methodology demonstrates a vastly superior computational cost-benefit ratio for practical drug discovery in low-data regimes.
