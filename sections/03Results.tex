\section{Results}

\subsection{Comparative Performance of Classical ML Across Feature Spaces}

To establish a rigorous baseline for TgDHFR inhibitor prediction, we evaluated classical machine learning ensembles across four distinct molecular representations using a \TotalOuterFolds-fold nested cross-validation framework. The purely structural representation utilizing Morgan fingerprints (ECFP4, \MorganNFeaturesTotal\ bits), devoid of additional physicochemical descriptors, yielded the highest predictive performance ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean \pm \MorganRTwoStd$, MAE = \MorganMaeMean). Within this feature space, a stacked ensemble comprising \MorganBestArchitecture\ emerged as the most frequently selected architecture during the inner cross-validation phase.

Increasing feature complexity failed to yield corresponding improvements in predictive accuracy. Integrating RDKit 2D descriptors with Morgan fingerprints (analogous to the original study's ``2D/FP'' model) produced an $R^2$ of $\RdkitTwoDFpRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDFpRTwoStd$ and an MAE of \RdkitTwoDFpMaeMean. The most complex representation, incorporating 3D geometric descriptors derived from ETKDGv3 embedding and MMFF94s optimization (analogous to the ``2D/3D/FP'' model), attained an $R^2$ of $\RdkitTwoDThreeDFpRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDThreeDFpRTwoStd$ and an MAE of \RdkitTwoDThreeDFpMaeMean. A paired t-test comparing the purely structural Morgan fingerprint ensemble against the highly engineered 2D/3D/FP model revealed no statistically significant difference in explained variance ($t = \PairedTTestStat$, $p = \PairedTTestP$). Although the mean absolute error for the 2D/3D/FP descriptor set appeared marginally lower than that of the pure Morgan ensemble, a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test across the \TotalOuterFolds\ outer folds confirmed this variance lacks statistical significance ($W = \WilcoxonMaeW$, $p = \WilcoxonMaeP$). These results indicate that conformational 3D featurization offers no measurable predictive advantage for this specific benchmark. Conversely, relying exclusively on 2D physicochemical descriptors severely degraded performance ($R^2 = \RdkitTwoDRTwoMean \pm \RdkitTwoDRTwoStd$, MAE = \RdkitTwoDMaeMean), confirming that topological fingerprints act as the primary drivers of predictive capacity.

To explicitly contextualize the computational cost-benefit ratio of advanced statistical techniques, we benchmarked our highest-performing classical model against the incremental progression reported in the original study (Table~\ref{tab:methodological_comparison}). Our classical stacking ensemble trained solely on Morgan features ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean$) performed comparably to their unselected 2D/3D/FP baseline model ($R^2 = \PaperBaselineRTwo$), exhibiting no statistically significant difference ($t = \TStatMorganVsPaperBaseline$, $p = \PValueMorganVsPaperBaseline$). 

However, this baseline classical approach fell short of the original study's final data-augmented deep neural network ensemble ($R^2 = \PaperFinalRTwo$, $t = \TStatMorganVsPaperFinal$, $p < 0.001$). These comparative results critically isolate the true source of predictive gains in this low-data regime. They demonstrate that achieving a competitive baseline performance ($R^2 \approx \PaperBaselineRTwo$) does not require computationally expensive 3D descriptors or complex neural network architectures; simple 2D fingerprints paired with classical machine learning algorithms suffice. Consequently, the performance gap between our baseline and the original study's final model directly quantifies the impact of synthetic data augmentation rather than inherent architectural superiority.

\subsection{Benchmarking Tree-Based Ensembles Against Graph Neural Networks}

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) represent the current state-of-the-art in molecular property prediction, leveraging message passing to learn representations directly from molecular graphs. However, their efficacy remains highly sensitive to dataset volume. To determine whether deep representation learning inherently provides a predictive advantage for this specific TgDHFR dataset (\NCompounds\ compounds), we benchmarked our highest-performing classical ensemble against a Directed Message Passing Neural Network (D-MPNN) implemented via the Chemprop framework.

The D-MPNN yielded an out-of-sample $R^2$ of \ChemPropDMPNNRTwoMean\ (95\% CI [\ChemPropDMPNNCILow, \ChemPropDMPNNCIHigh]) and a Mean Absolute Error of \ChemPropDMPNNMae. Despite its architectural sophistication, this performance is statistically comparable to the classical stacking ensemble utilizing pre-computed Morgan fingerprints ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean$). The failure of the GNN to significantly outperform the classical baseline is a direct consequence of data scarcity. With only \NCompounds\ training instances, the D-MPNN lacks sufficient data volume to learn graph convolutions that surpass the predictive capacity of fixed, topological ECFP4 descriptors. 

Crucially, the plateau of this advanced representation learning algorithm provides critical contextual validation for the methodologies employed by the original study \cite{karimova2025}. The observation that an unaugmented D-MPNN converges at an $R^2 \approx 0.75$ inherently explains why the original authors were compelled to deploy heavy statistical interventions—specifically, Gaussian data augmentation—to force a high predictive accuracy ($R^2 = \PaperFinalRTwo$, $p < 0.001$). These findings substantiate our central thesis: when evaluated on unaugmented, data-limited chemical spaces, architectural complexity alone fails to guarantee superior generalization. Instead, classical tree-based ensembles operating on fixed molecular descriptors remain the most robust, parsimonious, and computationally efficient choice. The comparative performance across all baseline models and the GNN is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:gnn_comparison}.

\subsection{Virtual Screening and Prioritization of FDA-Approved Candidates}

To identify potential repurposing candidates, the optimized classical machine learning model was deployed to screen a filtered library of FDA-approved drugs. The predictive reliability of the model was first validated against known TgDHFR inhibitors present in the dataset. The model demonstrated excellent agreement with experimental values, predicting a pIC$_{50}$ of 6.4226 for pyrimethamine (experimental: 6.56, absolute error: 0.1374) and 5.6590 for trimethoprim (experimental: 5.57, absolute error: 0.0890). 

The virtual screening prioritized candidates based on absolute predicted potency alongside established ligand efficiency metrics, including Ligand Efficiency (LE), Binding Efficiency Index (BEI), Lipophilic Ligand Efficiency (LLE), and Surface Efficiency Index (SEI). Table~\ref{tab:fda_candidates} details the top 10 predicted compounds. Antifolate analogs, including folic acid, methotrexate, and pralatrexate, exhibited the highest absolute predicted affinities (pIC$_{50} > 7.6$). However, absolute potency often scales with molecular weight, which can obscure the intrinsic binding quality of the scaffold. 

When evaluated through the lens of heavy-atom efficiency, smaller molecules demonstrated superior binding profiles. Pyrimethamine (LE = 0.52, BEI = 25.82) and the potassium-sparing diuretic triamterene (LE = 0.49, BEI = 27.09) emerged as highly efficient binders. Triamterene, in particular, represents a compelling repurposing candidate, as it achieves a high predicted pIC$_{50}$ of 6.8599 while maintaining exceptional ligand and binding efficiency indices, suggesting a highly optimized interaction with the TgDHFR active site relative to its molecular size.

\subsection{Integrated ML-Docking Consensus Filtering and False-Positive Mitigation}

To mitigate the false-positive rates inherent to standalone virtual screening methodologies, we implemented an orthogonal consensus filtering pipeline that integrates ligand-based machine learning predictions with physics-based molecular docking. We assessed the global agreement between the ML-predicted $pIC_{50}$ values and AutoDock Vina binding energies across the prioritized subset, yielding a Pearson correlation coefficient of $r = \DockPearsonR$ ($p = \DockPearsonP$). 

While the negative correlation correctly indicates that higher predicted potencies generally trend with stronger (more negative) binding energies, the lack of statistical significance ($p > 0.05$) across this highly restricted subset ($N = \DockNTotal$) is both expected and methodologically revealing. It underscores that 2D ligand-based machine learning and 3D physics-based docking capture fundamentally orthogonal aspects of molecular interaction. The ML ensemble interpolates bioactivity based on topological patterns learned from the training distribution, whereas Vina calculates thermodynamic stability based on spatial complementarity and electrostatic interactions. Consequently, relying on either method in isolation leaves the screening process highly susceptible to distinct classes of false positives. 

Crucially, our consensus approach effectively identified and eliminated these methodological artifacts. For example, compounds such as bisacodyl exhibited strong thermodynamic binding energies during structural validation ($\Delta G < -8.0\text{ kcal/mol}$), representing classic structure-based false positives often driven by the spurious accommodation of flexible, hydrophobic scaffolds within the binding pocket. However, our classical ML model correctly assigned a low predicted affinity to this compound, effectively vetoing it. By requiring dual validation, the integrated filter prevents the misallocation of experimental resources toward artifacts generated by single-method screening.

The orthogonal docking step successfully identified \DockNValidated\ consensus hits from the \DockNTotal\ evaluated compounds that satisfied both the ML potency threshold and stringent docking energy criteria. The most promising consensus candidate, \DockBestCandidate, demonstrated a strong ML-predicted $pIC_{50}$ of \DockBestMLpIC\ coupled with a highly favorable docking score of \DockBestDockScore\ kcal/mol. 

These results definitively establish that our lightweight, classical pipeline not only matches the statistical performance of unaugmented deep learning models but is also fully capable of discovering biologically viable and physics-validated inhibitors. By successfully isolating high-confidence repurposing candidates without relying on synthetic data generation or computationally expensive 3D feature engineering, this methodology demonstrates a vastly superior computational cost-benefit ratio for practical drug discovery in low-data regimes.
