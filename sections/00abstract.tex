\begin{abstract}
	Small bioactivity data sets make it easy to overstate the benefit of complex models, because of data leakage and because the model selection is rarely evaluated on data it did not influence. We re-examined this for inhibitors of \textit{Toxoplasma gondii} dihydrofolate reductase (TgDHFR, $N = \NCompounds$ curated compounds), comparing classical stacking ensembles on four molecular representations with a Directed Message Passing Neural Network (D-MPNN) on the same \TotalOuterFolds\ folds of a repeated nested cross-validation. Ensembles on 2D Morgan fingerprints reached $R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean \pm \MorganRTwoStd$ ($\text{MAE} = \MorganMaeMean$), and the best representation, RDKit 2D descriptors with fingerprints, $R^2 = \RdkitTwoDFpRTwoMean$. Adding 3D descriptors changed nothing detectable ($\Delta R^2 = \NBdiffRTwoTwoDFpVsTwoDThreeDFp$). The untuned D-MPNN reached $R^2 = \ChemPropDMPNNFoldRTwoMean \pm \ChemPropDMPNNFoldRTwoStd$, numerically lower but not significantly different from any classical representation after multiple-comparison correction (adjusted $p \geq \GnnVsMinPRTwo$); this is not evidence of equivalence, and the network was not tuned. Feature selection and Gaussian-noise augmentation, credited with large gains in the original study, did not improve a classical learner under its hold-out protocol, so those gains were not reproduced. The classical benchmark was not cheaper to run than the network's. Applying the Morgan ensemble to an FDA-approved library (\ScreenNLibrary\ compounds) and docking \ScreenNCandidates\ candidates with AutoDock Vina gave \AdmetNConsensus\ consensus hits, of which \AdmetNConsensusSurvivors\ passed in silico ADMET filters; agreement between predicted potency and docking energy was weak ($\rho = \DockSpearmanRho$), and selectivity over human DHFR was not assessed. For this data set, a fixed-fingerprint ensemble was sufficient, and the evidence does not support the added complexity of the models we compared; we make no claim beyond this target and regime.
\end{abstract}
