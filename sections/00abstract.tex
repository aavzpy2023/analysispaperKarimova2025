\begin{abstract}
	Predictive modeling in low-data bioactivity regimes faces significant methodological challenges, including high susceptibility to overfitting, silent data leakage, and unwarranted architectural complexity. Here, we present a leak-free benchmarking framework evaluating classical machine learning stacking ensembles against state-of-the-art Graph Neural Networks (ChemProp D-MPNN) for the inhibition of \textit{Toxoplasma gondii} dihydrofolate reductase (TgDHFR, $N = \NCompounds$). Utilizing a rigorous \TotalOuterFolds-fold nested cross-validation protocol, we systematically compared four molecular representation spaces spanning 2D topological fingerprints, 2D physicochemical descriptors, and 3D geometric conformers. Classical stacking ensembles trained on lightweight Morgan fingerprints achieved superior predictive performance ($R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean \pm \MorganRTwoStd$, MAE = \MorganMaeMean), statistically matching the performance of computationally intensive 2D/3D hybrid descriptors ($R^2 = \RdkitTwoDThreeDFpRTwoMean$, $p = \PairedTTestP$) while entirely eliminating the conformational overhead of 3D modeling. Conversely, the D-MPNN underperformed ($R^2 = \ChemPropDMPNNRTwoMean$, MAE = \ChemPropDMPNNMae), demonstrating that data scarcity restricts end-to-end representation learning. Deploying the optimized classical model to screen FDA-approved therapeutics prioritized high-potency antifolates alongside high-efficiency scaffolds, including triamterene (ligand efficiency = 0.49) and pyrimethamine (ligand efficiency = 0.52). Subsequent orthogonal validation via AutoDock Vina molecular docking resolved screening discordances, successfully filtering out false positives such as bisacodyl while confirming high-affinity consensus leads, most notably \DockBestCandidate\ (docking energy: \DockBestDockScore~kcal/mol). These findings demonstrate that in data-constrained QSAR regimes, the explicit inductive bias of fixed structural fingerprints acts as a natural regularizer that outperforms deep graph representations, providing a reproducible and computationally efficient paradigm for target-specific drug repurposing.
\end{abstract}


\textbf{Keywords:} 
	Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship, Low-Data Regimes, Nested Cross-Validation, Data Leakage, Graph Neural Networks, Molecular Docking
