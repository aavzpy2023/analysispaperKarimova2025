# Paper outline — *When Simple Is Enough: Leakage-Free Benchmarking Classical Machine Learning vs. Graph Neural Network*

One-glance map of the manuscript (`Classical_ML_vs_Graph Neural Networks.tex`, sections in `sections/`). Each section says **what was done and why**. Numbers are deliberately left out: they are generated by the pipeline and live in the paper (`latex/*.tex`).

**Question.** On a small bioactivity set (TgDHFR inhibitors, 859 curated compounds), do graph networks, 3D descriptors, feature selection and data augmentation buy anything over classical ensembles on fingerprints, once the evaluation is leakage-free?

**Status:** ✔ rewritten and checked against the code and results · ⏳ pending (waiting for new runs or not yet revised).

---

## 1. Introduction  ✔

- **1.1 Low-data regimes.** Small data sets invite overfitting; simple models often hold up. *Why:* frames the problem.
- **1.2 Engineered features vs. end-to-end learning.** Fingerprints/descriptors vs. GNNs and language models; the original TgDHFR study used Gaussian-noise augmentation and a DNN ensemble. *Why:* states the contested question.
- **1.3 Data leakage.** Preprocessing and model selection must stay inside the fitting loop. *Why:* motivates nested cross-validation.
- **1.4 Objectives and contributions.** Six contributions: leak-free paired evaluation, corrected tests plus equivalence (TOST), classical vs. default/tuned D-MPNN, replication of the original gains, robustness checks, screening as hypothesis generation. *Why:* states what was done without promising parity or validated leads.

## 2. Materials and Methods

- **2.1 Dataset and curation ✔.** Raw records → two extreme potency values excluded (Tukey fences) → duplicates collapsed by canonical SMILES. An ablation justifies the order of the steps. *Why:* a reproducible, auditable training set.
- **2.2 Representations ✔.** Morgan fingerprints, RDKit 2D descriptors, and their combinations with 3D descriptors (ETKDGv3 + MMFF). *Why:* separates what each feature block contributes.
- **2.3 Nested cross-validation ✔.** Repeated 5×5 outer folds; inner search over stacking ensembles of up to three learners; preprocessing fitted inside the folds; Nadeau–Bengio corrected tests with Holm adjustment. *Why:* no test-set leakage and valid hypothesis tests.
- **2.4 D-MPNN baseline ✔.** Chemprop network on the same outer folds: default hyperparameters, plus a small grid search nested in each fold. *Why:* a graph-network reference under identical conditions.
- **2.5 Screening and ligand efficiency ✔.** Final Morgan ensemble applied to the FDA library after size, element and Isolation-Forest domain filters; ranked by predicted potency and efficiency metrics. *Why:* turns the benchmark into a repurposing triage.
- **2.6 Docking ✔.** Redocking control, then AutoDock Vina on the candidates; consensus classes combine ML potency and docking energy. *Why:* an independent, structure-based opinion.
- **2.7 ADMET ✔.** Lipinski filter plus hERG and Caco-2 Random Forests trained on public data, on salt-free structures. *Why:* flags attrition risks early.
- **2.8 Environment and reproducibility ✔.** Seeds, determinism of each step, software versions, run times. *Why:* lets a reviewer rerun everything.

## 3. Results

- **3.1 Classical models across feature spaces ✔.** Comparison of the four representations and of the best architectures; fingerprints carry the signal. The original study's selection and augmentation gains are replayed on a classical learner. *Why:* tests whether those interventions transfer.
- **3.2 Classical ensembles vs. the graph network ✔** (default and tuned network). Paired, corrected and equivalence tests of the default and tuned D-MPNN against every representation, plus the measured run times. *Why:* the central benchmark.
- **3.3 Virtual screening ✔.** Funnel from the FDA library to the docked candidates; known inhibitors as a consistency check (they are in the training set, so it only checks fit). *Why:* shows what the model prioritizes and why efficiency matters.
- **3.4 ML–docking consensus ✔.** Rank correlation between predicted potency and docking energy, consensus hits and discordant cases. *Why:* reports agreement and disagreement instead of hiding it.
- **3.5 ADMET profiling ✔.** Lipinski, hERG and Caco-2 outcomes for the consensus hits and the known inhibitors. *Why:* shows which hits survive basic safety and absorption filters.
- **3.6 Robustness to scaffold-grouped splits ✔.** Morgan ensemble and D-MPNN re-run with Bemis–Murcko-grouped outer folds; both lose about 0.2 in $R^2$ and the families cannot be separated. *Why:* quantifies how optimistic random folds are.
- **3.7 Sensitivity to extreme potency values ✔.** The whole benchmark repeated with the two extremes kept, plus a diagnosis of how well they are predicted. *Why:* justifies excluding them as a domain decision, not a performance trick.

## 4. Discussion

- **4.1 Inductive bias in small data sets ✔.** Reading of the benchmark: no detectable gain from learning the representation; presented as an interpretation, with the modest network search and the missing learning curve as caveats. *Why:* keeps the conclusion inside what the data show.
- **4.2 Dimensionality and 3D overhead ✔.** What each feature block adds, and the measured cost of computing them; model fitting, not conformers, dominates the run time here. *Why:* tests the "parsimony" argument with measurements.
- **4.3 Scope, limitations, translational potential ✔.** Scaffold-split drop, activity cliffs, instability of the inner search, ADMET model limits, and the missing selectivity against human DHFR. *Why:* tells the reader how far the results can be trusted.
- **4.4 Conclusions and future work ✔.** Three conclusions (classical not worse, rigorous evaluation needed, screening gives hypotheses) and the next experiments. *Why:* closes without overclaiming.
